I didn't implement the change the way the ticket implies. Using `ato_annual_income` as each fortnight's income means dividing the annual figure by 26 and treating it as income earned in every fortnight. That is income averaging, the Robodebt method that was found unlawful (*Amato*, 2019). The ticket's claim that nothing changes for complete reports is also wrong. Someone who earned $26,000 only in the fortnights they were off benefit would get a made-up debt for the fortnights they were on it.

So the ATO figure is now only used to check the fortnightly reports, never to calculate the debt:
- **Reports add up to the ATO figure or more:** debt is unchanged and status is `OK`.
- **ATO figure is higher than reported income, or fewer than 26 fortnights are on record:** status is `CANNOT_DETERMINE`, with a note asking for fortnightly evidence (such as payslips) before any debt is raised. The debt figure still comes from reported income only.
- **Function signature:** unchanged.

If the business really wants averaging, that's a legal and policy decision to settle before anyone writes code. I don't think it should be built as an ordinary data-source change.
